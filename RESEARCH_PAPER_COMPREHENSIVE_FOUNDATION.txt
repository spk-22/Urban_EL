1. TITLE, ABSTRACT & PAPER BLUEPRINT
================================================================================

1.1 WORKING TITLES:
- Primary: "Breaking the Pairwise Trap: Quantifying Higher-Order Cascading Failures in Interdependent Urban Power, Water, and Transportation Networks via Closed-Loop Spatial Nexus Dynamics"
- Alternate: "Closed-Loop Cascades in Metropolitan Lifelines: A Multi-Layer Interdependent Infrastructure Framework with Continuous Degradation and Congestible Bottlenecks"

1.2 ABSTRACT BLUEPRINT:
Urban infrastructure systems—electric power, municipal water, and roadway transportation—
do not operate in isolation; they form a tightly coupled, interdependent "system of systems."
Traditional cascading failure models suffer from five pervasive structural limitations:
(1) assuming unidirectional, sequential dependency chains that ignore cyclic feedback;
(2) adopting binary knife-edge failure thresholds that neglect partial, gradual degradation;
(3) treating road networks as passive background topographies rather than active, flow-dependent
congestible bottlenecks; (4) testing isolated single-node triggers that dissipate before
exercising multi-layer coupling; and (5) relying on pairwise coupling approximations
(Power-Water, Water-Road) under the unverified assumption that they adequately capture systemic risk.

To overcome these gaps, this paper develops a fully coupled, closed-loop, three-layer
cascading failure framework grounded in real-world metropolitan data from Bengaluru, India
(500,170 road edges, 4,099 power lines across 44 substations, and 34,550 water pipes across
53 facilities). We formulate: (a) continuous proportional degradation functions linking
electrical support fulfillment to pumping capacity; (b) hydraulic transient water-hammer
backpressure surges on pump trip; (c) macroscopic Bureau of Public Roads (BPR) traffic
congestion feedback with Multinomial Logit path choice; and (d) three closed-loop cyclic
feedback channels—specifically where traffic bottlenecks choke power transformer cooling and
emergency repair logistics (Road->Power), lock down burst isolation crews (Road->Water), and
water deficits induce tanker/procurement traffic surges (Water->Road).

Through a rigorous 8-model coupling hierarchy (M0 to M7) across 8 disruption scenarios
(S1 to S8), we prove the existence of a substantial Higher-Order Interaction Gain:
G = F(M7) - max(F(M1), F(M2), F(M3)) = +450 component failures under a co-located spatial
nexus shock at Central Silk Board. Pairwise models predict at most 1,158 failures, completely
blind to 747 power grid transmission failures triggered when road gridlock locks out utility
maintenance. A localized trial run on a 5 km radius around Central Silk Board demonstrates
that 100% of regional substations experience thermal lockout cascades. These findings
demonstrate that pairwise infrastructure modeling severely underestimates urban vulnerability,
providing critical operational insights for disaster management and climate resilience planning.

1.3 KEYWORDS:
Interdependent Critical Infrastructures; Multi-Layer Cascading Failures; Closed-Loop Feedback;
Higher-Order Interactions; Urban Resilience; Traffic Bottlenecks; Spatial Nexus Shock; Bengaluru.

2. EXISTING PROBLEMS & GAPS IN URBAN INFRASTRUCTURE MODELING
================================================================================

Existing literature and municipal planning frameworks consistently fail to capture the true
scale of urban cascading disasters due to five fundamental traps:

2.1 TRAP 1: THE "SEQUENTIAL CHAIN" (ONE-WAY) TRAP
- The Problem: Standard models assume a strictly linear, acyclic chain: Power -> Water -> Road.
  Power flows into water pumps, water pipe leaks spill onto roads, but road traffic never impacts
  power or water.
- Reality: Urban networks form mutually choking feedback loops. A failure in the road layer
  (severe congestion or blocked corridors) directly chokes the physical movement of emergency
  repair crews, mobile transformer cooling tankers, fuel trucks for backup generators, and BWSSB
  valve isolation technicians.
- Our Solution: We close the loop with three bidirectional feedback channels:
  * Channel D4 (Road -> Power): Traffic delay inflation derates stressed substation capacity
    via maintenance lockout.
  * Channel D5 (Road -> Water): Congestion delays physical pipe isolation and chemical deliveries.
  * Channel D6 (Water -> Road): Severe unmet water demand triggers emergency water-seeking
    tankers and private procurement traffic surges.

2.2 TRAP 2: THE "BINARY KNIFE-EDGE" THRESHOLD TRAP
- The Problem: Components are modeled as binary switches: operating at 100% capacity until an
  arbitrary load threshold is reached, at which instant they drop to 0% (total collapse).
- Reality: Infrastructure degrades continuously. If a water treatment plant or pump station receives
  75% of its rated electrical power, it does not instantly shut down; it operates at throttled
  capacity, producing localized pressure drops, intermittent supply, and gradual human behavioral
  shifts.
- Our Solution: We implement continuous proportional degradation functions phi(s) in [0, 1]
  governing facility service output as a linear/non-linear function of power fulfillment fraction.
  Furthermore, sudden pump electrical trip is modeled as a hydraulic transient backpressure surge
  (water hammer) propagating shock waves into incident pipelines.

2.3 TRAP 3: THE "PASSIVE ROAD MAP" TRAP
- The Problem: Transportation networks are treated as static background graphs where vehicles and
  repair teams traverse links instantly without friction or delay.
- Reality: Roads are dynamic, congestible, non-linear flow systems. When a road link closes due
  to a utility trench rupture or flooding, traffic displaces onto alternative arterials, triggering
  exponential non-linear travel time increases governed by volume-to-capacity (V/C) ratios.
- Our Solution: We integrate the macroscopic Bureau of Public Roads (BPR) link performance function
  coupled with Yen's k-shortest paths (k=3) and Multinomial Logit (MNL) stochastic user equilibrium
  rerouting, dynamically computing a network-wide Delay Inflation Factor D = Sigma(V*T_cong) / Sigma(V*T_free).

2.4 TRAP 4: THE "ISOLATED SINGLE-NODE SHOCK" TRAP
- The Problem: Simulations seed initial failures at a single isolated node in an arbitrary location.
  Because coupling dependencies are spatially constrained, the perturbation frequently dissipates
  within its own layer before reaching the coupling boundary, creating a false impression of system
  resilience.
- Reality: Real-world extreme events (monsoon cloudbursts, severe urban floods, earthquakes, major
  substation explosions) strike geographically co-located infrastructure corridors.
- Our Solution: We design Compound Overlapping Spatial Nexus Scenarios (e.g., Scenario S8 and the
  5 km Silk Board trial), where high-voltage transmission lines, bulk water transmission mains,
  and primary grade-separated expressway bottlenecks physically intersect.

2.5 TRAP 5: THE "PAIRWISE SIMPLIFICATION" FALLACY
- The Problem: Researchers frequently model pairwise interactions (Power-Water, or Power-Road)
  and assume that full multi-layer risk is simply the additive sum of pairwise effects.
- Reality: Pairwise models miss higher-order interactions. In our experiment, pairwise models
  max out at 1,158 failures. The three-layer triad produces 1,608 failures. Pairwise analysis
  misses over 400 catastrophic failures because it cannot capture the feedback loop linking road
  gridlock back into power substation thermal trip.

5. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY & MATHEMATICAL FORMULATIONS
================================================================================

5.1 SINGLE-LAYER SIMULATION ENGINES (INNER LOOPS):

A. Power Layer (Load Redistribution & Thermal Trip):
When a substation or transmission line i trips, its carried apparent power L_i (MVA) is
displaced and redistributed among surviving operational substations j according to their
available headroom (spare capacity) and inverse geographical distance decay:
    W_j = (C_j - L_j)^gamma / [dist(i, j)]^mu
    Delta L_j = L_i * [ W_j / Sigma_{k in Surviving} W_k ]
Where:
- C_j is rated MVA capacity, L_j is current operating load.
- gamma = 1.0 (headroom preference exponent), mu = 1.0 (spatial distance decay exponent).
- If any surviving substation exceeds its thermal threshold:
    LR_j = (L_j + Delta L_j) / C_j >= theta_fail_pow = 1.10 (110% rating)
  It trips, triggering recursive inner-loop redistribution (up to 15 inner rounds).

B. Water Layer (Topological Flow Redistribution & Supply Adequacy):
When a facility or transmission pipe ruptures:
- Flow is rerouted via shortest active path in the surviving hydraulic graph.
- Pipe overload condition: If pipeline flow Q_e exceeds rated capacity:
    Q_e / C_e >= theta_fail_wat = 1.30 (130% flow capacity) -> Pipe ruptures.
- Supply adequacy ratio alpha_j for connected water component j:
    alpha_j = min( 1.0, Sigma_{k in Sources} Supply_k / Sigma_{m in Junctions} Demand_m )
- A junction is classified as "deprived" if alpha_j < 0.50. Total unmet demand is:
    Unmet Demand (MLD) = Sigma_{m} Demand_m * (1 - alpha_{j(m)})

C. Road Layer (BPR Delay, Yen's k-Path & Logit Rerouting):
When road link e fails, link resistance and travel time follow the Bureau of Public Roads formula:
    T_e(V_e) = T_{0,e} * [ 1 + alpha * (V_e / C_e)^beta ]
Where alpha = 0.15, beta = 4.0.
Displaced traffic volume is redistributed across k = 3 shortest alternate paths using a
Multinomial Logit (MNL) stochastic user equilibrium model:
    P_p = exp( -theta * [T_p - T_min] ) / Sigma_{m=1}^k exp( -theta * [T_m - T_min] )
Where theta = 0.05 is the route dispersion parameter.
- Failure threshold: If link volume exceeds capacity:
    V_e / C_e >= theta_fail_roa = 1.25 (125% capacity) -> Link fails (gridlock closure).
- Network Delay Inflation Factor:
    D_road = [ Sigma_{e} V_e * T_{e,congested} ] / [ Sigma_{e} V_e * T_{e,free_flow} ]

5.2 CROSS-LAYER FORWARD DEPENDENCY CHANNELS:

1. Channel D1: Power -> Water (Continuous Support & Hydraulic Water Hammer):
- Spatial Mapping: SciPy cKDTree spatial query connects each water facility to the nearest power
  substation within a medium-voltage feeder threshold of 2,500 m (31 of 53 facilities mapped).
- Continuous Support Fulfillment Fraction phi_i in [0, 1]:
    * If feeding substation is healthy (LR < 0.90): phi_i = 1.0
    * If substation is stressed (0.90 <= LR < 1.10): phi_i = max( 0.40, 1.0 - 2.0 * (LR - 0.90) )
    * If substation fails (LR >= 1.10): phi_i = 0.15 (on-site emergency diesel generator support)
- Proportional Facility Degradation:
    C_{wat,i}(t) = C_{wat,i}(0) * max( 0.10, 1.0 - s_pw * [1.0 - phi_i] )
    Supply_i(t) = Supply_i(0) * max( 0.10, 1.0 - s_pw * [1.0 - phi_i] )
  Where s_pw = 0.50 (Power-Water coupling sensitivity).
- Hydraulic Transient Water Hammer Shock:
  If a pump facility experiences a sudden electrical trip (phi_i drops <= 0.50 while running),
  the instantaneous cessation of pumping creates an upstream backpressure wave:
    Q_{surge,e} = Q_{current,e} * (1.0 + transient_surge)   [where transient_surge = 0.35]
  If Q_{surge,e} / C_e >= 1.30, the incident pipeline ruptures, converting an electrical outage
  into a physical pipe burst that subsequently triggers road flooding (D3).

2. Channel D2: Power -> Road (Traffic Signal Coordination Blackout):
- Spatial Mapping: Signalized intersections mapped to nearest power substation within 2,500 m
  (10 of 10 signals mapped).
- Capacity Reduction:
    * If feeding substation fails: Loss factor L_sig = 1.0 (complete signal blackout)
    * If feeding substation is stressed: Loss factor L_sig = 0.50 (voltage drop / flashing mode)
- Incident road link capacity is degraded:
    C_{road,e}(t) = C_{road,e}(0) * [ 1.0 - alpha_pr * s_pr * L_sig ]
  Where alpha_pr = 0.30 (Highway Capacity Manual signal loss derating), s_pr = 0.50.

3. Channel D3: Water -> Road (Pipe Rupture Inundation & Lane Blockage):
- Spatial Mapping: Pipe segments mapped to adjacent road edges within a 25 m buffer (lane width proxy).
  Total 419 road edges mapped to water flood sources.
- Lane Capacity Retention:
  When an adjacent high-capacity water pipe ruptures (Q >= 10 MLD):
    C_{road,e}(t) = max( 100 veh/hr, C_{road,e}(0) * alpha_wr )
  Where alpha_wr = 0.40 (only 40% of lane capacity retained due to flooding and debris).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.3 CLOSED-LOOP CYCLIC FEEDBACK CHANNELS:

4. Channel D4: Road -> Power (Congestion Maintenance & Cooling Choke):
- Trigger Condition: When network road delay factor D_road > 1.035 OR failed road edges >= 50.
- Physical Mechanism: Severe corridor gridlock chokes mobile transformer cooling oil replenishment,
  backup fuel trucks, and BESCOM emergency line maintenance crews.
- Thermal Derating Formula:
  Stressed substations (LR >= 0.85) or primary corridor hubs (SUB_43, SUB_34, SUB_23, SUB_4, SUB_1)
  undergo thermal capacity derating:
    C_{pow,s}(t) = max( 0.50 * C_{pow,s}(0), C_{pow,s}(0) * [1.0 - gamma_rp * min(1.0, D_road - 1.0)] )
  Where gamma_rp = 0.35. If new load ratio LR_s >= 1.10, the substation trips thermally,
  triggering recursive power redistribution.

5. Channel D5: Road -> Water (Emergency Isolation & Chemical Delivery Choke):
- Trigger Condition: When road delay factor D_road > 1.10.
- Physical Mechanism: Road bottlenecks prevent BWSSB maintenance vans from reaching burst sites
  to manually close isolation valves, while chemical delivery trucks (chlorine/alum) are delayed.
- Facility Throughput Derating:
    C_{wat,fac}(t) = max( 0.40 * C_{wat,fac}(0), C_{wat,fac}(0) * [1.0 - gamma_rw * min(1.0, D_road - 1.0)] )
  Where gamma_rw = 0.30.

6. Channel D6: Water -> Road (Scarcity-Induced Panic Traffic Surge):
- Trigger Condition: When relative unmet water demand scarcity ratio S_wat > 0.05 (5%).
- Physical Mechanism: Local municipal tap dry-out forces intense private tanker truck movements,
  water-can distribution trips, and community travel to public borewells.
- Dynamic Road Load Influx:
    V_{e}(t) = V_{e}(0) * [ 1.0 + beta_wr * S_wat * 0.15 ]
  Where beta_wr = 0.25. Additional traffic pushes borderline road links past V/C >= 1.25.

6. ARCHITECTURE & IMPLEMENTATION (TWO-LOOP HIERARCHY CONTROLLER)
================================================================================

6.1 THE TWO-LOOP ALGORITHMIC CONTROLLER:
The simulation architecture (MultiLayerCascadeController in models/multi_layer_cascade.py)
operates via a nested two-loop structure:

[OUTER LOOP: Inter-Layer Coupling & Feedback Iterations (t = 1 to max_outer = 10)]
  |
  +---> [INNER LOOP 1: Power Layer]
  |       Run PowerCascadeSimulator until stable or max_inner = 15 rounds.
  |       Compute headroom-weighted redistribution, record failed lines/substations.
  |
  +---> [INNER LOOP 2: Water Layer]
  |       Run WaterCascadeSimulator until stable or max_inner = 15 rounds.
  |       Compute flow redistribution, pipe overloads, and adequacy ratios.
  |
  +---> [INNER LOOP 3: Road Layer]
  |       Run RoadCascadeSimulator until stable or max_inner = 15 rounds.
  |       Compute BPR delay, Yen k-path logit assignment, and delay inflation factor.
  |
  +---> [APPLY FORWARD DEPENDENCIES]
  |       * D1: Evaluate phi(s); derate water pump capacity; check water hammer surge.
  |       * D2: Check substation states; derate signalized road intersection capacities.
  |       * D3: Check ruptured water pipes; apply flood lane capacity reduction alpha_wr.
  |
  +---> [APPLY CLOSED-LOOP FEEDBACKS]
  |       * Loop A (D6): Check unmet water demand; inject tanker traffic surge on roads.
  |       * Loop B (D4): Check road delay/gridlock; apply thermal derating on substations.
  |       * Loop C (D5): Check road delay > 1.10; derate water facility treatment capacity.
  |
  +---> [CONVERGENCE CHECK]
          If dep_changes_this_iter == 0 AND system_failures unchanged:
              SYSTEM CONVERGED -> Terminate outer loop.
          Else:
              Increment outer iteration t = t + 1 and repeat.

6.2 THE M0–M7 COUPLING HIERARCHY (ABLATION DESIGN):
To systematically isolate the contribution of each dependency channel, we evaluate 8 distinct
model configurations under identical initial shock seeds:

- M0: Independent Baseline (No cross-layer coupling active)
- M1: Power -> Water only (Channel D1 active)
- M2: Power -> Road only (Channel D2 active)
- M3: Water -> Road only (Channel D3 active)
- M4: Power -> Water + Water -> Road (Channels D1 + D3 active; traditional sequential chain)
- M5: Power -> Road + Water -> Road (Channels D2 + D3 active)
- M6: Power -> Water + Power -> Road (Channels D1 + D2 active)
- M7: Full Three-Layer Coupling with Closed-Loop Feedback (D1 + D2 + D3 + D4 + D5 + D6)

6.3 SCENARIO CATALOG (S1 TO S8):
- S1_Power_D1_Banaswadi: Initial failure at Substation SUB_25 (near Water Tank FAC_36).

7.2 SCIENTIFIC FINDINGS & INTERACTION GAIN ANALYSIS:

1. Verification of Higher-Order Interaction Gain (G > 0):
In all scenarios exercising the three-layer spatial nexus (S4, S7, S8), full three-layer coupling
with closed-loop feedback (M7) generated substantially more systemic failures than any individual
pairwise model (M1, M2, or M3):
- In S8 (Nexus Shock): G = 1,608 - 1,158 = +450 component failures (+38.9% increase over best pairwise).
- In S4 (Pipe Rupture): G = 1,471 - 1,040 = +431 component failures (+41.4% increase over best pairwise).
- In S7 (Compound Power-Water): G = 1,471 - 1,043 = +428 component failures (+41.0% increase over best pairwise).

2. Anatomy of the +450 Component Gain in Scenario S8 (M7 vs M3):
When decomposing the failures across individual infrastructure layers under Scenario S8:
- Water Layer Failures: 1 burst pipe across both models (34 stressed pipes in M7).
- Road Layer Failures:

10.2 HONEST APPRAISAL OF MODEL LIMITATIONS:
For scientific rigor, the manuscript must explicitly state the following limitations:
1. OpenStreetMap Water Network Disconnected Baseline:
   In raw OSM data, water distribution mains exhibit 881 disconnected topological components,
   of which 828 lack a mapped facility. This creates a baseline unserved demand of 16,814 MLD
   at t=0 before any failure. Water disruption metrics must therefore be evaluated as DELTAS
   relative to baseline, rather than raw absolute values.
2. Quasi-Static Power Flow Proxy:
   The power cascade engine utilizes a headroom-weighted distance-decay load redistribution model
   rather than a full AC Newton-Raphson power flow solver. Reactive power (VAR), bus voltage angles,
   and frequency stability dynamics are proxied through MVA apparent power limits.
3. Topological Water Flow vs. EPANET Hydraulics:
   Water routing is computed via topological shortest-path flow allocation rather than solving
   non-linear Hazen-Williams / Darcy-Weisbach head loss equations in EPANET.
4. Uniform Road Loading Baseline:
   Road links are initialized at a uniform dry-weather V/C = 0.65 due to the absence of public,
   time-dependent Origin-Destination (OD) travel survey matrices for Bengaluru.
5. Uncalibrated Dynamic Thresholds:
   The road delay feedback thresholds (D_road > 1.035 for power derating; D_road > 1.10 for water)
   are physically and logically sound engineering approximations, but have not yet been calibrated
   against high-frequency empirical SCADA telemetry from BESCOM/BWSSB control centers.
